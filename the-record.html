Strategic Plan, Followed
Transportation

Getting people around the city.

Density where the transit already is, last-mile options for the rest of the trip, and honest plans for the roads.

5 items

Transportation was one of the main things I ran on in 2022, and it's stayed at the top of the list. Some of what we promised is done, some is happening this year, and some is still in the planning stages.

Returning Summer 2026
The Shoreline Shuttle is coming back.

Bringing it back was something I committed to in 2022, and the buses are returning this summer.

In Place
E-bike and e-scooter program.

A real last-mile option for the trip between transit and home, brought in by this council and being used every day.

Built
Multi-use path along St. Johns.

The first stretch is in. More of it is coming as the city grows.

In Planning
Widening the bridge at Barnet and Ioco.

It's a real congestion problem at that intersection, and there's a real plan to take it on.

In Planning
HOV lane on St. Johns during rush hour.

It has to be paired with the bridge widening, otherwise it just creates a new bottleneck. The two go together.

Community Builds

Places people actually use.

The kind of community spaces you only really notice when they aren't there.

4 items

Honestly, this council got more built in the last four years than the two terms before us put together. It wasn't luck. It came from staying focused, agreeing on a plan, and sticking to it.

Built
The new boardwalk.

Done, open, and being used by everyone from morning runners to families on a Saturday.

Completed
Inlet Field, dedicated to Bob Favelle.

Finally finished after years of waiting, and named for someone who put a lot into this community.

Ground Broken
New field house, under construction.

It had been in planning for years. Ground broke in 2024 and it's now being built.

Phase 2 in Progress
Kyle Centre redevelopment.

The next phase of the redevelopment is happening now.

Governance

The boring stuff that actually matters.

A plan you can hold council to, and a strategy for how the city grows.

3 items

A lot of the most important work in city government is invisible until you don't have it. The pieces this council got into place make the next decade easier to manage.

Adopted
Council Strategic Plan.

Four big priorities, all agreed to by nearly every member of council, and actually followed through on: sustainable core services, healthy community development, a resilient natural environment, and a vibrant, prosperous community.

Adopted
Land Management Strategy.

A first for Port Moody. It gives the city a real plan for how city-owned land is managed, instead of figuring it out one parcel at a time.

Repaired
Relationships across the region.

A lot of working relationships had frayed before 2022. The ones with the business community, with non-profits, with the school district, and with our neighbouring municipalities have all been put back together.

Engagement

Getting more voices into the room.

Especially the ones that usually get missed.

2 items

Council decisions are better when more people are part of them. A couple of concrete things have happened over the last few years to actually make that real.

Hosted
Two Youth Summits.

We brought the youth voice directly to council, twice. It worked, and it's something I want to keep doing.

Established
Mayor's Task Force on Arts and Business.

It's now turning into an arm's-length Arts and Business Council that brings the two communities together. The city is helping it get on its feet with seed funding and a space to work from.

Fiscal Responsibility

Protecting the services people count on.

Being smart with money isn't really about cutting. It's about asking whether there's another way first.

2 items

When the easy answer is to cut a service that people rely on, the harder and better question is whether there's a way to bring in new revenue instead.

In Place
Pay parking in the busiest areas.

Not everyone was happy about it, and I understand that. But it brought in revenue so we didn't have to cut services, and it took some of the pressure off the parking situation at the same time.

Ongoing
Conservative, audited budgets.

Fifteen years chairing the Finance Committee is what Meghan brings to every line of the budget.

The Place Itself

This is what we're working for.

The inlet, the pier, the mountains, the trails. Every decision council makes either protects this or chips away at it. The work is worth doing because the place is worth keeping.

“We did more in the last four years than the previous two terms put together.”

Meghan Lahti
Keep It Going

There's still a lot to finish.

The shuttle is coming back. The bridge widening is in planning. So is the HOV lane. The field house is being built. The Arts and Business Council is just getting off the ground. The housing work is in motion. All of it needs continuity to actually land, and that's what I'm asking voters for.
