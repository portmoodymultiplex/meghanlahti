Meet Meghan

Experience that brings people together.

Nearly thirty years of service means knowing the people, the files, and the work already underway. But experience only matters when it helps people work together, make sound decisions, and keep the community at the centre.

Outside the council chamber, I spent close to twenty years in post-secondary education. Most of what I know about this place, though, I learned the ordinary way: on the sidelines, at the food bank, and at a lot of kitchen tables.

The priorities

Four things that guide the work.

Not new ideas invented for a campaign. They are the continuation of a plan this Council built together and reported on in public. Here is where each one stands.

01

A City That Moves

Getting around Port Moody should be easier, and the plan for it has an order.

Traffic is real, and there is no single fix. The credible part is the sequence, so each piece makes the next one work instead of moving the bottleneck down the road.

Completed

Shuttle and last mile. The Shoreline Shuttle returns this summer. The e-bike and e-scooter program and the St. Johns multi-use path are in place.

Next

The bridge at Barnet and Ioco. Widening it comes first, because it is the real congestion point.

Then

A rush-hour lane on St. Johns. It works once the bridge is done, not before.

02

Growth We Can Be Proud Of

Growth should give us neighbourhoods people are glad to live in.

This is not development for the sake of development. People in Moody Centre have asked for a grocery store and walkable services for years. Newport Village and Suter Brook were contentious when they were proposed, and now they are places much of Port Moody is happy to call home.

Moderate growth means putting most new homes near transit, so established low-rise neighbourhoods can remain low-rise.
Housing that works for
Renters First-time buyers Families sizing up Seniors downsizing near family Below-market and affordable

What is affordable to one person is out of reach for another, and no single type of housing answers every need.

03

Value for Every Tax Dollar

Being careful with money starts with protecting what people count on.

Protect core services

Keep the services residents rely on reliable, and look for revenue and savings before cutting them.

Plan for maintenance and renewal

Account for the long-term cost of roads, pipes, and buildings before they turn into emergencies.

Broaden the tax base

Grow the business and appropriate industrial base, so homeowners are not carrying the city alone.

The proof, in one number

The 2026 tax increase came in at 3.95 percent, the lowest of this Council's term, brought down from a proposed 8.65 percent through savings and new revenue.

04

Good Governance That Works

Different perspectives. Public decisions. One shared plan.

Good governance starts with relationships, and over this term the working relationships a city depends on were rebuilt and strengthened.

Relationships this Council worked to strengthen
Residents Businesses Community organizations Neighbouring municipalities Provincial and federal partners

The mayor is one vote. The job is to create the conditions for good decisions to be made.

Council is made up of different people with different views, and they do not always agree. That is healthy. Disagreement is not division. Everyone deserves room at the table, and out of that work this Council agreed on a shared direction and made its decisions in public. Good governance depends on trust, fairness, experience, and collaboration, not on any one person deciding alone.

Leadership begins with listening.

People can disagree without the community being divided. Good leadership creates room for different perspectives, helps people understand one another, and keeps decisions focused on the people who live here.

The mayor's role is not to make every decision alone. It is to bring people together, build working relationships, and help Council find a responsible path forward.
