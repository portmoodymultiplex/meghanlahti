The full guide is 58 pages. This is the practical breakdown for our campaign — what we need to do, what we can't do, and when things are due.

## 1. The Calendar (General Voting Day: Saturday, October 17, 2026)

| Period | Dates | What's regulated |
|---|---|---|
| **Election period** | Jan 1 → Sept 18, 2026 | All election expenses must be tracked & disclosed. Not subject to the expense limit. |
| **Pre-campaign period** | July 20 → Sept 18, 2026 (89 days before GVD → 29 days before GVD) | Election **advertising** is regulated. Sponsorship/authorization tags required on ads. |
| **Campaign period** | Sept 19 → Oct 17, 2026 (28 days before GVD → close of voting) | Subject to the **expense limit**. Hard cap, monetary penalties + loss of seat if exceeded. |
| **Filing deadline** | Jan 15, 2027 (90 days after GVD) | Disclosure statement due to Elections BC. |
| **Late filing deadline** | Feb 14, 2027 (120 days after GVD) | Last day to file with $500 late-filing fee. Miss this = disqualified. |
| **Records retention** | Through Oct 17, 2031 | All campaign financing records kept in BC for 5 years after GVD. |

Quick translation: the only window where there's a *spending cap* is the last 28 days. Everything before that (Jan 1 → Sept 18) is regulated for advertising sponsorship and disclosure but has no dollar ceiling. The ceiling itself (the campaign-period expense limit for Port Moody mayor) will be published by Elections BC by **May 31, 2026**.

## 2. Contributions — Who, How Much, How

### Who can give
- Only **eligible individuals**: BC residents who are Canadian citizens or permanent residents.
- **Prohibited**: corporations, unions, out-of-province donors, anonymous online contributions, contributions routed through someone else's name.

### How much they can give
- Annual contribution limit is CPI-adjusted and published on the Elections BC site (`elections.bc.ca/local-contributions`). Most recent figure we've been working with: **$1,429.70 per eligible individual per calendar year, per jurisdiction**.
- The limit covers monetary + in-kind + loans, combined.
- Endorsed candidates share the limit with their elector organization (not applicable to us — Meghan is independent).
- **Election-year rule for unendorsed candidates**: Meghan can contribute up to **double** the annual limit to her own campaign in 2026.

### How they give
- Contributions go to the financial agent or a person authorized in writing by the financial agent. Anyone collecting money who isn't authorized = the contribution is prohibited and must be returned.
- Date of contribution = date received, not date deposited.
- Online contributions: the donation form must have a checkbox where the donor declares eligibility.
- Payment processor fees count against the donor's limit. If someone donates the max online and Stripe takes $2, that $2 is technically a prohibited contribution. Worth keeping the max donation a couple of dollars below the cap.
- **Anonymous**: allowed only up to $50 per person, total. Must be truly anonymous (pass-the-hat at an event). Cannot be collected online.

### Required donor info
Every contribution gets recorded with name, address, value, date, and how it was received — even if under $100 and not publicly disclosed. Records are still required.

## 3. Expenses — What Counts, What Doesn't

### Three buckets

**A. Election expenses subject to the campaign-period limit** (the hard ceiling):
- Campaign advertising — signs, brochures, newspaper ads
- Reused/repurposed signs from previous elections (valued at current market replacement cost)
- Transmission costs — postage, website hosting, etc.
- Paid photographers, videographers, ad production
- Campaign-related meetings, rallies, functions
- Paid research, polling, phone banks
- Paid canvassing (in person, phone, internet)
- Campaign office costs
- Bank fees

**B. Election expenses NOT subject to the limit** (still reported):
- Refunded sign/nomination/damage deposits
- Candidate's personal expenses reimbursed from the campaign account
- Legal or accounting services for LECFA compliance
- Financial agent services
- Interest on permissible loans
- Brochures printed but never distributed (intended election expenses → "other expenses")

**C. Not election expenses at all**:
- Goods Meghan made from property she already owned before deciding to run
- Volunteer time and volunteer-owned property (canvassing, sign installation, running social media from their own computer)
- Free media coverage (interviews, news stories, debates)
- A publication that was being sold regardless of the election

### Valuation rules
- Use price paid OR market value, whichever is higher.
- Reused signs from a previous campaign: get a current quote from a sign maker and report that as the expense. (Yes, even though we didn't pay anything this cycle.)
- Donated goods: market value, reported as both a contribution and an expense.

### Pre-approval & payment
Every expense must:
1. Be **pre-approved** by the financial agent before being incurred.
2. Be paid by the financial agent or a written-authorized person.
3. Be paid or reimbursed **through the campaign account**.

If Meghan pays for something with her personal card and isn't reimbursed, that becomes a contribution from her — and counts against her own contribution limit (which is doubled in election year, but still a limit).

## 4. Election Advertising — The Sponsorship Tag

From **July 20, 2026** onward (pre-campaign + campaign periods), every piece of paid election advertising must carry sponsorship/authorization info:

> Authorized by [Financial Agent Name], [BC phone, email, or mailing address]

This goes on:
- Signs, billboards, posters, branded merch
- Newspaper / radio / TV / magazine ads
- Pay-per-click, Facebook ads, promoted posts, banner ads
- Mailers, brochures, newsletter inserts
- Automated phone calls (robocalls)
- Paid canvassing scripts

What's NOT election advertising:
- Free social media posts (organic Facebook, Instagram, X, YouTube)
- The campaign website itself (the production cost is an expense, but the platform is free)
- Free media (interviews, news stories)
- Personal messages, private emails, in-person/phone conversations

**Third-party rule**: candidates cannot coordinate with third-party sponsors on advertising. We can't have someone else run "supporting" ads in coordination with us.

## 5. The Financial Agent

A candidate must appoint a financial agent in writing (or act as their own — Meghan can do either). The agent is responsible for:

- Keeping all financial records
- Opening the dedicated campaign account (separate account for every campaign, in the name of the campaign — e.g., "Meghan Lahti 2026 Port Moody General Local Election Campaign Account")
- Confirming every contributor is eligible
- Ensuring the contribution limit isn't exceeded
- Routing all money through the campaign account
- Pre-approving expenses
- Putting sponsorship info on advertising
- Filing the disclosure statement by Jan 15, 2027
- Responding to any Elections BC follow-up

Failure to comply = administrative monetary penalty paid personally by the financial agent in some cases, with their name published.

**Before voting results are declared**: appointment changes go to the local election officer (Port Moody City Hall).
**After results declared**: changes go to Elections BC.

## 6. Campaign Account Rules

- One dedicated bank account per campaign (mayoral race only here — straightforward).
- Account name must reference the election ("Meghan Lahti 2026 Port Moody General Local Election Campaign Account").
- ALL monetary transactions go through it: contributions in, expenses out, transfers.
- Stays open until surplus funds are disbursed.
- Credit card purchases allowed only if reimbursed from the account; otherwise they're contributions from whoever paid.

If a bank gives you grief opening the account, Elections BC has a letter on their website you can print and hand them.

## 7. Fundraising Functions

- Tickets only purchasable by eligible individuals, not reimbursable.
- Ticket purchases $50 or under: not a contribution.
- Ticket purchases over $50: counts as a contribution against the limit.
- All event income goes into the campaign account first — you can't pay the venue out of the cash box on site.
- Items donated for auction: market value = contribution from the donor. Sale over market value = additional contribution from the buyer.
- Need to track attendance count for the disclosure form.

---

## 8. Loans & Debts

- Loans from eligible individuals or savings institutions are allowed.
- A loan from an individual counts toward their annual contribution limit in the year received.
- Unpaid loans at filing become contributions — which can push someone over the limit retroactively. Watch this.

## 9. Disclosure Statement — What We File Jan 15, 2027

The disclosure statement (often called the Campaign Financing Report) covers:
- All campaign contributions (with donor info for amounts over $100)
- All election expenses, split by election period vs campaign period
- All transfers
- Surplus funds disposition
- Fundraising function details
- Signed by Meghan and her financial agent

Filing methods, in order of preference:
1. **SOFA** (Secure Online Filing Application) at services.electionsbc.gov.bc.ca/Portal — preferred
2. Email to electoral.finance@elections.bc.ca
3. Fax to 1-866-466-0665
4. Mail to PO Box 9275 Stn Prov Govt, Victoria, BC V8W 9J6 (postmark NOT acceptable as proof — must arrive by deadline)
5. Courier or in-person to Suite 100 – 1112 Fort Street, Victoria

### Consequences of missing the filing deadline
- Name published on Elections BC's website
- Local jurisdiction notified
- If elected: **loss of seat + by-election triggered**
- Disqualified from running until after the next general election
- Fine up to **$10,000 and/or 2 years imprisonment**
- $500 late fee buys 30 extra days (Jan 16 → Feb 14, 2027), but miss that and the above kicks in

### Supplementary report
If we discover an error or omission after filing — or Elections BC asks for one — we have **30 days** from notification to file a complete supplementary report.

### Public information
After the filing deadline, the disclosure statement is published on Elections BC's website. Donor addresses and phone numbers get redacted. Financial agent contact info is NOT redacted — it's part of the public record.

## 10. The Penalty Stack (why we follow this stuff)

If a candidate exceeds the campaign-period expense limit:
- Monetary penalty
- **Loss of seat if elected**
- Commits an offence

If a candidate accepts prohibited contributions:
- Monetary penalty per violation
- Excess must be returned/remitted

If sponsorship info is missing from an ad:
- Monetary penalty

If the disclosure statement isn't filed by Jan 15, 2027:
- See section 9 above. Worst case in the whole guide.
