The Platform

What I'm running on.

There are five things I want to focus on over the next four years. They're the same things I've been working on for most of my time on council, and they're the issues people in Port Moody actually talk to me about at the grocery store and on the trails.

01 · Transportation

Getting around Port Moody should be easier.

More transit options where they make sense, and honest road improvements where the congestion actually is. Both pieces matter, and we're working on both.

  • The Shoreline Shuttle is coming back this summer.
  • E-bike and e-scooter program in place for the last mile.
  • Multi-use path along St. Johns, with more to come.
  • Widening the bridge at Barnet and Ioco. HOV lane on St. Johns during rush hour.
Read more ›
02 · Housing & Affordability

Affordable housing comes in all forms.

Affordability isn't one thing. A first-time buyer, a family trying to upsize, someone wanting to downsize and stay close to their grandkids, a renter, a senior on a fixed income, they all need something different. A real housing plan respects that.

  • Seniors housing is a priority going into 2026.
  • Smart growth means density near transit, not high-rises everywhere.
  • Walkable neighbourhoods connected to services and amenities.
Read more ›
03 · Good Governance & Transparency

A council that works together.

When this council came together in 2022, we sat down and built a strategic plan nearly all of us could agree on, and then we followed it.

  • Strategic Plan adopted and followed through.
  • Land Management Strategy adopted, so growth is planned, not piecemealed.
  • Relationships repaired with businesses, non-profits, the school district, and neighbouring municipalities.
  • Two Youth Summits to bring the youth voice to council.
Read more ›
04 · Experience as an Asset

Experience is a good thing.

There's a lot you only learn after years at the council table. The relationships and the institutional memory take time to build. Meghan brings nearly three decades of that, including 16 years on the Metro Vancouver Board and 15 years chairing the Finance Committee.

  • Relationships across the region and across the country.
  • Context from years of decisions at the table.
  • A track record of working collaboratively.
Read more ›
05 · Fiscal Responsibility

Spending that protects services.

Being responsible with the city's money usually means looking for new revenue before cutting services. Pay parking in the busy areas is a good example. Not everyone loved it, but it brought in new revenue so services didn't have to be cut, and it helped with congestion at the same time.

What We've Done

A few of the things this council got done.

Some of these are finished. Some are just getting going. All of them are things this council has either built, started, or kept moving over the last four years. There's more, and the longer version with the context lives on the What We've Done page.

Transportation
The Shoreline Shuttle is coming back.

It's been off the road for years. We're bringing it back this summer.

Transportation
E-bike and e-scooter program.

A real last-mile option for the trip between transit and home, in place and being used.

Transportation
Multi-use path along St. Johns.

Built, and there's more of it on the way.

Governance
Land Management Strategy.

The first of its kind for Port Moody. It gives us a real plan for how city-owned land gets managed, instead of figuring it out one parcel at a time.

Community
The new boardwalk.

Built, open, and being used.

Community
Inlet Field, finished and dedicated to Bob Favelle.

A long-overdue project, completed and named for someone who put a lot into this community.

Community
New field house, under construction.

After years in planning, ground has broken and the build is happening.

Community
Kyle Centre, Phase 2.

The next phase of the redevelopment is under way.

Engagement
Two Youth Summits.

We brought the youth voice directly to council, twice, and it's something I want to keep doing.

Arts & Business
Mayor's Task Force on Arts and Business.

It's now turning into an arm's-length Arts and Business Council that brings the two communities together. The city will help it get on its feet with seed funding and space.
