I love this town.

In 1996, that was my whole campaign sign. Years of service later, it is still the reason I do this.

See the priorities Volunteer

Leadership begins with listening.

People can disagree without the community being divided. That is normal in a city that cares about where it is headed. My job as mayor is to make sure residents get a real chance to be heard, and to create room for different points of view at the table, including the ones I don't share.

Good decisions come out of that work. When people feel understood, Council can find a responsible path forward instead of forcing everyone into the same answer. Every one of those decisions comes back to the same test: is it right for the people who live here.

Meet Meghan

Nearly thirty years, one town.

I have served Port Moody since 1996, and in 2022 I became the city's first female mayor. Outside the council chamber, I spent close to twenty years in post-secondary education. But most of what I know about this place I learned the ordinary way, on the sidelines, at the food bank, and at a lot of kitchen tables. Experience is a good thing, mostly because it means you already know the people, the files, and how to get something finished.

A City That Moves

Getting around Port Moody should be easier, and the plan for it has an order.

Traffic is real, and there is no single fix. The credible part is the sequence, so each piece makes the next one work instead of moving the bottleneck down the road.

Completed

Shuttle and last mile. The Shoreline Shuttle returns this summer. The e-bike and e-scooter program and the St. Johns multi-use path are in place.

Next

The bridge at Barnet and Ioco. Widening it comes first, because it is the real congestion point.

Then

A rush-hour lane on St. Johns. It works once the bridge is done, not before.

Growth We Can Be Proud Of

Growth should give us neighbourhoods people are glad to live in.

This is not development for the sake of development. People in Moody Centre have asked for a grocery store and walkable services for years. Newport Village and Suter Brook were contentious when they were proposed, and now they are places much of Port Moody is happy to call home.

Moderate growth means putting most new homes near transit, so established low-rise neighbourhoods can remain low-rise.

What is affordable to one person is out of reach for another, and no single type of housing answers every need.

Value for Every Tax Dollar

Being careful with money starts with protecting what people count on.

Protect core services

Keep the services residents rely on reliable, and look for revenue and savings before cutting them.

Plan for maintenance and renewal

Account for the long-term cost of roads, pipes, and buildings before they turn into emergencies.

Broaden the tax base

Grow the business and appropriate industrial base, so homeowners are not carrying the city alone.

The proof, in one number

The 2026 tax increase came in at 3.95 percent, the lowest of this Council's term, brought down from a proposed 8.65 percent through savings and new revenue.

Experienced Leadership That Brings People Together

A city runs on relationships, and those take time to build.

Municipal leadership depends on all of these
Residents Council Businesses Local organizations Neighbouring municipalities Other levels of government

Over this term, those working relationships were rebuilt and strengthened. Council is made up of different people with different views, and they do not always agree. What changed is that people got room to be heard, and out of that the whole Council agreed on one shared plan and followed it.

The decisions are made in public, on the record. Good governance depends on collaboration, on staying consistent, and on staying accessible, which is why the mayor's door stays open to residents who want to sit down and talk.
