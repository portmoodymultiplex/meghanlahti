Meet Meghan

Nearly thirty years of service means knowing the people, the issues, and the work already underway. But experience only matters when it helps people work together, make sound decisions, and keep the community at the centre.

Outside the council chamber, I have spent my time working in post-secondary education and as a business owner. Most of what I know about this place, though, I learned the ordinary way: as a community volunteer, Girl Guide leader, soccer mom and Rotarian.

I love Port Moody. That is why I have spent most of my adult life in service to this community. It is where my husband and I chose to raise our family, and it is a place I am proud to have had the opportunity to help shape into the community we all know and love.

Four Priorities

01

A City That Moves

Getting around Port Moody is the number one thing people raise with me, and I do not blame them.

Traffic on St. Johns is not working for us. Over this term we have tried smaller fixes, and now it is time for a real vision for the street.

The Shoreline Shuttle is back on the road this summer, the e-bike and e-scooter program is in place, and the multi-use path on St. Johns is built, with more to come.

We widen the bridge at Barnet and Ioco first, then add the rush-hour lane on St. Johns, in that order, because the bridge has to come first or the lane simply moves the problem down the road.

St. Johns deserves a real plan, not another band-aid, and that is what we are building.

02

Growth We Can Be Proud Of

I am not interested in growth for the sake of growth.

I watched Newport Village and Suter Brook go from contentious to beloved, and the lesson stayed with me: building something the community is proud of requires active listening, a commitment to putting people first and genuine collaboration. Moody Centre is our chance to get this right, and I want to see the right growth for Port Moody there. The community has waited a long time for the shops and services that will come with this vibrant neighbourhood enhancement.

I believe in moderate growth. To me, that means placing density near transit and ensuring that other forms of housing, such as townhomes, low-rise housing and infill development, can take place throughout the rest of the city.

Housing has to work for everyone, from first-time buyers to seniors sizing down near their grandkids.

We have a solid plan for providing housing for families, and we have placed a high priority on ensuring affordable housing is built in this community through our inclusionary zoning policies, recent changes to our bonus density program and our ACC program. We still have a gap when it comes to seniors housing.

We need to ensure there is housing for all seniors: those who are downsizing and seeking market housing opportunities, as well as those who require affordable seniors housing. This is a priority.

03

Value for Every Tax Dollar

Responsible financial management is about more than keeping one year's property tax increase down.

It means protecting the services people rely on while preparing Port Moody for a sound financial future. It also means the services people count on actually working, from swimming lessons to field space to the everyday business of dealing with the city.

It is also a priority to keep diversifying City revenues by attracting business and growing our commercial and industrial tax base, so homeowners are not carrying the City's needs on their own.

That requires aligning service levels with financial planning, planning for asset lifecycle and renewal costs, and making sure the City can maintain the infrastructure and facilities residents depend on.

We look for revenue and savings before cutting core services. That same discipline helped bring this year's budget increase down from where it began and resulted in the lowest increase of the term.

04

A Council That Works Together

When I became mayor, there was important work to do rebuilding relationships with the business community, local, provincial and federal partners, non-profits and neighbouring municipalities. That work came first.

Council is made up of people with different perspectives. I created an environment where those voices could be heard, and we worked together to agree on one strategic plan, revisited it every year and followed it through.

We do not always agree on how to get there, but disagreement is not division.

What we have built is a council that works together, and Port Moody needs a mayor who can work with anyone. I have shown that I can.

Leadership is not a solo exercise. It is about seeking to understand what people are saying, creating space for them to articulate their positions, bringing people together and driving consensus.

We make decisions together, in public, and my door stays open to any resident who wants to sit down. That progress is worth protecting, and we cannot afford to slide backwards. That is what good governance looks like.

Top Priorities for the Next Term

  1. Building seniors housing
  2. A corridor vision for St. Johns, including Phase 2 completion, widening the Barnet overpass and increasing lane capacity
  3. Civic daycare facility completion
  4. Arts and Business Council
  5. Kyle Centre rebuild and the creation of a new park in Moody Centre
