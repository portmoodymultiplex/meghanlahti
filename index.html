Meet Meghan

Nearly thirty years of service means knowing the people, the files, and the work already underway. But experience only matters when it helps people work together, make sound decisions, and keep the community at the centre.

Outside the council chamber, I spent close to twenty years in post-secondary education. Most of what I know about this place, though, I learned the ordinary way: by raising my three kids here, school pick ups, drop offs, at the soccer field and in service to our community.

The plan

Four Priorities

01

A City That Moves

Getting around Port Moody is the number one thing people raise with me, and I do not blame them.

The Shoreline Shuttle is back on the road this summer, the e-bike and e-scooter program is in place, and the multi-use path on St. Johns is built, with more to come.

Next is the congestion itself. We widen the bridge at Barnet and Ioco first, then add the rush-hour lane on St. Johns, in that order, because the bridge has to come first or the lane simply moves the bottleneck.

Anyone promising a quick fix for St. Johns has not read the file.

02

Growth We Can Be Proud Of

I am not interested in growth for the sake of growth.

I watched Newport Village and Suter Brook go from contentious to beloved, and the lesson stayed with me: building something the community is proud of requires active listening, a commitment to putting people first and genuine collaboration.

Moody Centre now has the critical mass needed to support the grocery store and walkable services residents have asked for over many years.

Moderate growth means density near transit. Most new homes go where the buses and trains already run, so low-rise neighbourhoods stay low-rise. Housing has to work for the whole range, from first-time buyers to seniors sizing down near their grandkids.

03

Value for Every Tax Dollar

Responsible financial management is about more than keeping one year's property tax increase down.

It means protecting the services people rely on while preparing Port Moody for a sound financial future.

That requires aligning service levels with financial planning, planning for asset lifecycle and renewal costs, and making sure the City can maintain the infrastructure and facilities residents depend on.

We look for revenue and savings before cutting core services. That same discipline helped bring this year's budget increase down from where it began and resulted in the lowest increase of the term.

It is also a priority to keep diversifying City revenues by attracting business and growing our commercial and industrial tax base, so homeowners are not carrying the City's needs on their own.

04

Good Governance That Works

When I became mayor, there was important work to do rebuilding relationships with the business community, local, provincial and federal partners, non-profits and neighbouring municipalities.

That work came first.

Council is made up of people with different perspectives. I created an environment where those voices could be heard, and we worked together to agree on one strategic plan, revisit it every year and follow it.

We do not always agree on how to get there, but disagreement is not division.

Municipal leadership is not a solo exercise. It is about understanding what people are saying, creating space for them to articulate their positions, bringing people together and driving consensus.

We make decisions together, in public, and my door stays open to any resident who wants to sit down.

That is what good governance looks like.

What this Council built.

None of it was done alone. Council agreed on a plan, some of the work is finished, more is underway, and some is still ahead. The work isn't finished, and it is all public record.

  • The new boardwalk. A waterfront walk that gets used every day, from morning runners to weekend families. Completed
  • Bob Favelle Field. The rebuilt Inlet Field, finished and named for a long-serving member of the community. Completed
  • Council Strategic Plan. One shared plan for the term, agreed to by nearly all of Council and followed through. Completed
  • The new field house. In planning for years, now being built. Underway
  • The Shoreline Shuttle. Local shuttle service coming back after years off the road. Underway
  • Barnet and Ioco bridge widening. The next major piece of the transportation plan, still ahead. Coming next
