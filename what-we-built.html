The Record.

One plan, followed in public, and a list you can check. Council agreed on a plan, some of the work is finished, some is underway, and some is still in planning. None of it was done alone, and it is all public record.

Filter by status
Getting around the city

Transportation was one of the main things Meghan ran on in 2022, and it has stayed at the top of the list.

The Shoreline ShuttleUnderway

Bringing the shuttle back was a 2022 commitment, and the buses are returning this summer.

E-bike and e-scooter programCompleted

A real last-mile option for the trip between transit and home, in place and being used.

Multi-use path along St. JohnsCompleted

The first stretch is built, with more of it planned as the city grows.

Widening the bridge at Barnet and IocoIn planning

A real congestion point, with a real plan to take it on. It comes before the St. Johns lane, because order matters.

Rush-hour lane on St. JohnsIn planning

Paired with the bridge widening, so it eases the bottleneck instead of moving it.

Places people use

The kind of community spaces you only really notice when they are not there. Most of these had been talked about for years before this term.

The new boardwalkCompleted

Built, open, and used every day, from morning runners to families on a Saturday.

Inlet Field, named Bob Favelle FieldCompleted

Finished after years of waiting, and named for a long-serving, dedicated member of the community.

The new field houseUnder construction

In planning for years, ground has broken, and it is now being built.

Kyle Centre redevelopment, Phase 2Under construction

The next phase of the redevelopment is under way.

How the city is run

A lot of the most important work in city government is invisible until you do not have it. These pieces make the next decade easier to manage.

Council Strategic PlanCompleted

A shared plan for the term, agreed to by nearly every member of Council and followed through: reliable core services, healthy community development, a resilient natural environment, and a prosperous community.

Land Management StrategyCompleted

A first for Port Moody. It sets a plan for how city-owned land is managed, instead of deciding one parcel at a time.

Rebuilt working relationshipsOngoing

Relationships with the business community, non-profits, neighbouring municipalities, and community partners were put back on solid ground.

More voices at the table

Council decisions are better when more people are part of them, especially the voices that usually get missed.

Two Youth SummitsCompleted

The youth voice brought directly into Council chambers, twice, and something Meghan wants to keep doing.

Arts and Business CouncilUnderway

The Mayor's Task Force on Arts and Business is becoming an arm's-length council that brings the two communities together, with city support to help it start.

Careful with money

Being smart with money is less about cutting and more about asking whether there is another way first.

Pay parking in the busiest areasCompleted

Not everyone was happy about it. It brought in revenue so services did not have to be cut, and it took some pressure off parking at the waterfront at the same time.

Conservative, audited budgetsOngoing

Spending decisions that look for revenue and savings before reaching for the service axe, and that plan for the long-term cost of maintaining what the city owns.

What is next

Most of this has a next phase.

The shuttle is coming back. The bridge widening and the St. Johns lane are in planning. The field house is being built. The Arts and Business Council is getting on its feet. Finishing this kind of work is what the next four years are about.
